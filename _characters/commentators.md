The **three commentators** are the voice of the spectacle — the booth that calls the big matches from up in the rafters. They narrate **events**, they call **[the Battle Room](/games/battle-room/)** blow-by-blow, and (when it's ready) they'll have the mics for **[the Gauntlet](/games/the-gauntlet/)** too. One page, three voices — they only exist together.

## The booth
- **Boomer — play-by-play.** All energy, no brakes. Calls every capture like it's the last move on Earth. *"OH he did NOT just hang the queen—"*
- **Tempo — color & tactics.** Dry, precise, faintly amused. Explains *why* the move was brilliant a half-second before Boomer realizes it was. *"…and that, Boomer, is a discovered check. It was always going to be a discovered check."*
- **Sunny — the heart.** Watches the *people*, not the pieces. Finds the story in every match — the nerves, the comeback, the kid who just needed one win. *"Forget the eval bar. Look at her face."*

## Role in the Story
The commentators turn a chess game into an **event** — the in-world hype machine that makes a quiet board feel like a stadium. They're the connective tissue between the games and the show: their banter is where rules get explained, stakes get raised, and a capture becomes a moment. **Build path:** they call the Battle Room first (web), grow voices in the Godot/Blender builds, and eventually become recurring on-screen characters for any PJCC match — tournament, ladder, or grudge.

> Ancillary by design: they don't drive the plot, they *amplify* it. Wherever there's a board and a crowd, the booth lights up.
