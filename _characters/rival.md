**Michael** is the **Chess City rival** — introduced as an antagonist, revealed to be one of the most important allies in the story, and the show's truest moral compass. His name carries its meaning — *"Who is like God?"* — because Michael is the character who, again and again, **acts as God would: truly righteous.** Not self-righteous, not preachy — *righteous*, in the old sense: he does the right thing when it costs him, and he keeps doing it.

## Traits
- Same age as the narrator; paired with the **king** piece
- Intensely driven by chess — it is his only road back to his family
- Tough exterior that softens once common ground is found
- A skilled teacher; introduces Princess to the **Pirc Defense**
- **Truly righteous** — the moral compass of the story (distinct from the *self*-righteous older brother)

## Background
He lives in Checker Town, but his entire family is in Chess City. That separation is the engine behind everything he does. Every game he plays, every lesson he gives, is a step closer to being reunited with the people he loves. He and the narrator start as rivals but find that they want the same things — and Michael's instinct, every time, is to do right by the people around him.

## Role in the Story
When the narrator is sent away, Michael steps up — taking in Princess and continuing her chess education. His arc moves from adversary to unlikely cornerstone of the group. He is a **side character** whose quiet, costly righteousness sets the story's moral center. The Bob-Proctor-flavored mindset line — *"goals are 5% strategy, 95% mindset"* — belongs to the Checker Town rival, [Robert](/characters/robert/); Michael's creed is simpler: *do the right thing, then the next one.*

> **Theme:** Michael is *truly* righteous (he acts as God), while the self-righteous older brother falls — true vs. counterfeit righteousness, side by side.
