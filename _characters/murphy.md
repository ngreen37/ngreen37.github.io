**Murphy** is the family's pessimist — named for **Murphy's Law** (*whatever can go wrong, will*). Where the Uncle carries Neville's belief and [Robert](/characters/robert/) carries the gospel of mindset, Murphy carries the dread: he is *certain* Chess City is no promised land but a nest of **evil chess pieces** — pieces with mouths and eyes, watching from behind the gates. Everyone calls him paranoid. In **[his game](/games/murphys-law/)**, he's right.

## Traits
- **Thinks ahead — but only on defense.** He calculates every way a thing can fail, and almost none of the ways it can go right.
- His worry is a real radar: it **catches threats other people walk straight into** — the loose plank, the ambush, the trap that smiles.
- The same worry is a blind spot: head down bracing for the hit, he **misses the open door, the free square, the kindness right in front of him.**
- Speaks in warnings and worst-cases; his catchphrase is a grim little *"...told you."*
- Plays as a **pawn** — the piece most sure it's about to be sacrificed, and the one that quietly guards the most.
- Warm underneath the gloom. The dread is love wearing armor: he frets because he can't stand to see anyone get hurt.

## Background
Murphy grew up on the same Checker Town streets as his brothers, but where they heard *"believe and go,"* he heard *"believe and get burned."* He is one of the wary — the ones who never crossed the sea, who trade the Whisper of evil pieces gate to gate like scripture. He didn't choose fear; fear chose him early, and it has, on occasion, been *correct* — which is exactly what makes it so hard to put down. He stands at the gate with a packed bag and a list of everything that could go wrong, and he is not entirely wrong about any of it.

## Role in the Story
Murphy is the show's **doom-sayer and its loving foil** — the shadow side of the Neville-Goddard optimism the rest of the journey runs on. His comedy and his heart come from the same gag: his worst fears are **vindicated on-screen** (the pieces really are grinning at him) while the world around him stays stubbornly warm. He's the playable lead of **[Murphy's Law](/games/murphys-law/)**, the Mega-Man-style scroller whose tagline says it all — *"The pessimist was right."* His arc is the slow, tender argument that being right about the danger is not the same as being right about the world.

## In the Academy
Murphy is the Academy's **defense coach** — and its best cautionary tale. His one good habit is the one every player needs: before you move, **ask what your opponent is threatening.** Murphy *always* asks. He sees the fork, the back-rank mate, the trap two moves out, because he's already living in the version where it happens to him.

But Murphy only ever sees that half of the board. So afraid of what could go wrong, he **misses what could go right** — the winning sacrifice, the open file, the move that wins because it's brave, not safe. That's the lesson the Academy teaches *through* him:

- **Borrow Murphy's eyes for threats** — *checks, captures, threats* before every move.
- **Then put them down** — once you've seen the danger, look for your own chance. Caution that never turns into a plan just loses slowly.

> Murphy keeps you alive. He won't help you win. That part's on you.

## A word from Murphy
