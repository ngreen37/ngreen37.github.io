**Robert** is the **Checker Town rival** — the local hotshot who treats the board like a stage and the mind like a muscle. He talks like **Bob Proctor**: all mindset, manifestation, and unshakeable certainty. Where Michael is quietly righteous, Robert is *loud* about belief — and, to everyone's irritation, often right.

## Traits
- A Checker Town local, same generation as the narrator — a friendly rival, not a villain
- Speaks in **mindset axioms**: his signature line is *"goals are 5% strategy, 95% mindset"*
- Coaches as much as he competes — can't resist turning a loss into a lesson
- Plays a **bishop**: works the long diagonals, always pushing an angle
- Genuinely wants you to win — *after* he does

## Background
Robert grew up on the same Checker Town streets as the narrator, but somewhere along the way he got religion about *belief itself* — that the game is won between the ears before a single piece moves. He's the one telling you to picture the win, assume the feeling, and "act as if." It would be insufferable if it didn't keep paying off. He's part of the show's spiritual debate — the Neville-Goddard / Bob-Proctor energy made into a rival you actually like.

## Role in the Story
A **side character** and a foil: Robert is the *mindset* rival to Michael's *moral* one. Together the two rivals frame the show's question — is the journey won by being right, or by being righteous? Robert pushes the narrator to believe bigger; Michael shows him what to do with the belief. Robert's two brothers — **[the Prodigal Brothers](/characters/prodigal-brothers/)** — are a cautionary echo of where mindset goes when the heart isn't in it.
