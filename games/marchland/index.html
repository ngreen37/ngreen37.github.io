Your round has three parts. Deployment — place every new rank yourself. Attack — up to three a round. Fortify — one free move of ranks between two holdings you hold. Then you watch them take theirs, step by step.

Ranks are dice. One die per two ranks, up to four, and you keep the best. Under three ranks you cannot field a queen at all. And the Chain: a holding with two or more neighbors of your own is chained, its borders light up in your color, and it rolls an extra die every time it defends.

The dice are spent, never compared. One roll buys material at real chess values — queen 9, rook 5, bishop or knight 3, pawn 1, kings free. The other places it: a high roll advances the attacker's pawns, or digs the defender's into a chain and tucks their king into the corner.

Twenty seconds each. Only the side to move burns clock, and every move gives back up to a second of what it cost — so moving quickly costs you nothing. Run your own clock out and you lose. Checkmate takes the ground outright; a draw goes to the defender.
