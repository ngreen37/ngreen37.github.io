The duel. Someone lays a real opening trap and says something about the position. You answer by picking a line at the bottom — each one is a real move. First to three exchanges takes the bench.

You learn by losing. Walk into a trap and it goes in your notebook as one you can spring. Spring one and get answered, and you learn their comeback. The duels you lose teach you the most, which is the whole idea.

The Swordmaster opens once you have collected every comeback. She plays openings you have never seen — and the answers you learned on the bench still fit. That rhyme has a name in chess: a transposition.

Nothing here is invented. Every line is a real trap, re-proved against the site's referee on every push — legal from move one, and any mate you are threatened with proved forced against every defense.

A homage. Duel Mode is a love letter to insult swordfighting in The Secret of Monkey Island (Ron Gilbert, Lucasfilm Games, 1990) — the duel where you fight with words, you can only learn a comeback by having it used on you, and the Sword Master says things nobody has ever said to you before. That structure is theirs and it is brilliant. The board, the traps, the writing and the code here are ours; no art, code or dialogue was taken.
And the insults are about the position, never about the person holding it.
