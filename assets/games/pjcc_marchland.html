How it works

1. Pick a fight. Tap one of your holdings, then a neighboring enemy one. You need a garrison of 2 or more to attack.

2. Both sides roll two twenty-sided dice. One decides your material, the other your position. Nothing is compared — the dice are spent.

3. Material buys an army. Every point buys chess material at its real value: queen 9, rook 5, bishop or knight 3, pawn 1. Kings are free — they always turn up. The defender gets four points of home ground on top.

4. Position places it. A high roll advances the attacker's pawns; a high roll digs the defender's into a chain their neighbors defend. On defense, 16 or better tucks your king into the corner — and 18 or better brings his rook around and three pawns with him.

5. Twenty seconds against fifteen. Two clocks, and only the side to move is burning one. Checkmate takes the ground outright. If the attacker's clock runs out the attack failed; if the defender's does, the ground changes hands. A draw goes to the defender — ground is easier held than taken.

6. The winner takes the holding. Lose them all and the campaign is over.
