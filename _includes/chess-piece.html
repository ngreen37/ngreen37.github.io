(2026-07-22) ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Nate: "Regarding the Chess pieces, I meant actual pieces, that a human would use on a physical chessboard. The symbols look out-of-place." He is right, and the reason is that Unicode ♛♚♝ are TYPE. They are cut to sit in a line of text beside letters, so they carry a font's own idea of weight and detail, they change shape on every machine (Segoe here, Apple Symbols on a Mac, DejaVu on Linux), and at 40px against a lit sky they read as printer's marks lying on the picture rather than as objects standing in it. These are Staunton profiles: base, collar, stem, and a head that names the piece. HOW THE OUTLINE WORKS, and why nothing here is stroked. A piece is built from several overlapping shapes, and stroking shapes would draw a line around every one of them — every internal seam, the exact hollow line-art look Nate asked me to get rid of. Instead the contour is a stacked `drop-shadow` in the CSS, which filters the RENDERED RESULT: it sees one merged silhouette and traces its outside edge only. Solid colour inside, one line around the outside, nothing in between. That is also why the bishop's slit is a NOTCH cut into the mitre's edge rather than a drawn line — on a real piece it is a gap, so here it is a gap in the silhouette, and the contour follows it in for free. Colours come from the chess canon tokens (_sass/_pjcc-22-chess-canon.scss), never from here, so these can never drift from the boards.
