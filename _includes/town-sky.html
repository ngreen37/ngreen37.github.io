{%- comment -%} ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ THE TOWN SKY — the whole town lives under one sky, on every page. ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── 2026-07-12 (Nate: "the other pages, I thought we were going to give them day/dusk/night/dawn themes, like the P&JCC page? Let's really make that a thing"). Up to now the hour existed in exactly two places: the P&JCC hero's full sky, and a 0.03–0.07 color wash over everything else — technically town-wide, invisible in practice. This is the real thing: a fixed, full-viewport sky sitting BEHIND every page (z-index:-1, so it paints over the flat body color and under every card, header and footer). You see it in the gutters, above and below the content — the town is now a place the pages sit inside, instead of a color they're tinted with. It's STATIC MARKUP, not JS-built: `sky-` and `cloud-` are already on before first paint (see town-weather.html), so the sky is correct on the very first frame with no flash and no layout work. Stacking, and it matters — Nate: "the stars should always fall BEHIND the moon. Unless it's a shooting star": stars (1) → orb, sun or moon (2) → shooting stars (3) → clouds (4) So a shooting star can streak across the moon's face, and cloud can pass in front of the lot. Nothing else may sit between the stars and the orb. PERF (the 2026-07-11 lag rules, which this obeys to the letter): the container is position:fixed + overflow:hidden + contain, so it is capped at the VIEWPORT and never grows with the document. Every moving part animates transform/opacity ONLY — no background-position anywhere, no filter on any wrapper of an animating child. Off entirely under reduce-flourish / prefers-reduced-motion, which leaves the static sky (gradient + orb) exactly as it is. ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 1 · the stars. Night only. Two of every handful twinkle; the rest are fixed points, because a sky where every star pulses reads as a screensaver. {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 1b · VAGUE CONSTELLATIONS (2026-07-15 Nate: "P&JCC-universe constellations … no pressure to get it right. There is no right"). Night only, and shown at RANDOM per visit — the script at the foot rolls how many appear: 10% all · 50% two · 30% one · 10% none. Faint dots + fainter joining lines that only SUGGEST the shape. Order here is irrelevant; the roll shuffles them. {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 2 · the orb: one element, four looks. The day sun grows rays; the night moon gets a crater shadow. Sits ABOVE the stars, always. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} 3 · shooting stars. Night only, and the one thing allowed in FRONT of the moon. Three of them on long, deliberately coprime cycles (29s · 43s · 67s) with a streak that lasts barely a second — so they never fall in a pattern you can read, and most of the time the sky is simply still. No JS: the sky is idle markup and the compositor does the rest. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} The wrapper (2026-07-21) exists so the SKY VEIL has something to fade. Each streak's own opacity is driven by its keyframes, so setting opacity on the itself would be overridden mid-flight; the veil rides the group instead and the two compose. It also carries the z-index:3 the streaks used to hold, so the stars → orb → shooting stars → clouds order is unchanged. {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 3b · THE SEASON (2026-07-15 Nate: "a set P&JCC calendar … subtle things to signal the season"). `season-` is on before paint (town-weather.html). A faint per-season tint, plus a handful of drifting particles: leaves in fall, snow in winter, petals in spring, fireflies on a summer NIGHT. Transform/opacity only, viewport-capped, off under reduced-motion / reduce-flourish. {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 4 · the cloud deck. Cover is a daily roll from the town clock (PJCC_TIME.clouds() → html.cloud-0..3), so the whole town gets the same sky, and it's decorrelated from the rain roll — a clear day can still be a cloudy one, and a starry night can still have cloud crossing it. Two banks drifting at different speeds (transform only) so the deck never repeats visibly. {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 4b · the shape clouds (2026-07-13 Nate: "like when you are with a friend pointing at clouds — 'that one looks like a rook'"). Two of them: a rook and a dog, VAGUELY — soft puffs that only suggest the outline. Rare twice over: each only exists on ~1 day in 4 (shape-rook / shape-dog, rolled from the same day seed as the rest of the weather in town-weather.html, so the whole town points at the same cloud), and within the day each spends half its long cycle off-screen. Parked at left:100% so with animation off they simply never enter. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} 4c · the LONG-cloud day (2026-07-15 Nate: "sometimes the clouds get really loooong, like clouds do — a little more transparent than the fluffy ones"). Two stratus streaks, gated by html.shape-long (~1 day in 4). {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 5 · the far horizon: the town's own light, low and warm, along the foot of the sky. It's what stops the backdrop reading as "a gradient" and starts it reading as "somewhere". {%- endcomment -%}
{%- comment -%} 6 · THE ECLIPSE VEIL (2026-08-09) — the daylight going out. Its opacity is the sun's coverage, so it deepens and lifts with the eclipse instead of switching. ⚠ z-index 0, which puts it UNDER the stars (1) and the orb (2) on purpose: the sky has to darken while the corona stays the brightest thing on the screen, and stars coming out in the middle of the afternoon is the whole payoff. {%- endcomment -%}
